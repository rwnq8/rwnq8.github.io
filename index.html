I study autonomous AI systems as systems: how their parts interact, where they fail, and what keeps them legible and accountable to the people who rely on them.

I have worked on transportation networks and systems since my first job. That thread runs through my whole career, and it is why I work on autonomous AI as a systems researcher rather than as a software engineer: my work is system design, modeling, evaluation and oversight.

  • Network modeling, from regional to national. I began as a GIS analyst (Cross County Connection TMA), then a systems planner (Delaware Valley Regional Planning Commission) and a transportation consultant (IBI Group), working on transportation networks and regional systems planning. At the U.S. Federal Highway Administration I led technical components of the national Tour-Based Model System for long-distance passenger travel, and managed a $1.5M federal research portfolio as a certified Contracting Officer's Representative.
  • Data-driven decisions at national scale. At Deloitte I delivered analytics and machine-learning engagements for federal and public-sector clients. At AARP's Public Policy Institute I led the Livability Index, which combines 50+ data sources across 7 domains to score U.S. neighborhoods and inform policy decisions.
  • Autonomous systems research at QNFO. I designed and operate an autonomous AI research system that drafts, referees, revises, publishes and corrects papers. I treat it as a research object: what it delivers, what it costs, how it fails, and how its claims stay auditable.
  • Current questions: how do people keep oversight of autonomous AI systems that act on their own? How should such a system state what it does not know? What does a correct answer from an agentic system cost, in energy, for the whole system?
